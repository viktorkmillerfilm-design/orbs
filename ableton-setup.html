orbs → ableton live, on a mac

a short setup so the solar app can play instruments in ableton live over midi. use chrome or edge (firefox desktop is fine too). safari does not support web midi, including on iphone and ipad, so the midi button will say midi n/a.

if chrome blocks midi on a file:// url, serve the folder from a terminal:

python3 -m http.server

then open http://localhost:8000 and grant the midi permission when chrome asks.

1. turn on the iac driver

this is macos's built-in virtual midi cable, so orbs and live can talk on the same computer.

  1. open audio midi setup (in /applications/utilities, or spotlight it).
  2. choose window → show midi studio (or press ⌘2).
  3. double-click iac driver and tick device is online.
  4. click + to add a second port. name them orbs notes and orbs clock. (one port is enough to get started; two ports keep clock and notes from interfering.)

2. tell live which ports to listen to

in ableton: settings / preferences → link, tempo & midi, then the midi ports list.

  • in: orbs notes (or “iac driver bus 1”) — track on, remote on, sync off. track lets notes through; remote lets you midi-map knobs and faders.
  • in: orbs clock — track off, remote off, sync on, only if you want orbs to drive live's tempo.
  • leave sync out off on those ports unless live is the clock master.

never turn sync on for both in and out of the same port — that can loop midi clock back into itself. only enable sync in on one port.

3. make one midi track per planet

create eight midi tracks and name them after the planets. load a drum rack or an instrument on each.

track midi channel typical note
kick 1 36 (c1)
snare 2 38 (d1)
hats 3 42 closed, 46 open
perc 4 45 / 48 / 50
bass 5 low bass notes
pad 6 chord tones
chords 7 chord tones
lead 8 melody notes

recorded planets (rec 1 … rec 8) use channels 9–16 and note 60 (c3). add those tracks when you need them.

on every track:

  1. midi from = orbs notes (the iac port).
  2. channel = the number in the table, not “all”.
  3. monitor = in (this is enough to hear it). arm the track only when you want to record a clip.

4. connect orbs and play

open orbs in chrome, click midi, and choose the orbs notes port (the picker prefers an iac port automatically). press play. you should see the tracks light up in live.

if a note hangs, click panic (or stop in orbs). that sends all-notes-off and all-sound-off on every channel.

5. map volume, pan, and fx (optional)

orbs also sends control changes on each planet's channel:

  • cc 7 volume · cc 10 pan · cc 74 filter cutoff · cc 71 resonance · cc 91 reverb send · cc 94 delay send

to map a live control: press ⌘m (midi map), click the fader or knob in live, then move the matching control in orbs. press ⌘m again to exit. save the set so the mappings stick.

if an instrument already reacts to cc 7 or 10, don't also map live's track volume or pan to the same cc — the two will stack.

6. sync tempo (optional)

if orbs is the clock master: in live, enable sync in on orbs clock, press ext on the transport, then play in orbs. live's tempo follows. if the metronomes don't quite line up, unfold that port in live's midi preferences and nudge midi clock sync delay. it may take a bar or two to settle.

if you'd rather live be the master, that comes later — for now, start orbs first.

7. record a clip

arm the tracks, record in live while orbs is playing, then set each clip's loop length to that planet's orbit (1 beat, 2 beats, 1 bar, 2 bars, or 4 bars).
